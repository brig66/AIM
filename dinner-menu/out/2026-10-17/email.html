Chimichurri Sirloin Steaks

Seared sirloin with a bright, garlicky parsley-oregano sauce. Ready in about a quarter of an hour, with one skillet and one bowl.
Protein: Beef sirloin (steak) Servings: 2 (for two)
Difficulty: Easy Cleanup: Easy (one skillet, one bowl)
Prep 10 min  |  Cook 10 min  |  Rest 5 min  |  Total 25 min
Ingredients for two
Chimichurri
  • 1/2 cup packed fresh flat-leaf parsley leaves, finely chopped
  • 1 tablespoon fresh oregano leaves, chopped (or 1/2 teaspoon dried oregano)
  • 1 medium garlic clove, finely minced
  • 2 tablespoons red wine vinegar
  • 1/4 cup extra-virgin olive oil
  • 1/4 teaspoon red pepper flakes
  • 1/4 teaspoon kosher salt
Steak
  • 2 boneless sirloin steaks, about 1 inch thick (about 6 ounces each)
  • 1/2 teaspoon kosher salt
  • 1/4 teaspoon black pepper
  • 1 tablespoon neutral oil (such as avocado or canola)
Directions
  1. Make the chimichurri first. Stir the parsley, oregano, and garlic together in a small bowl. Add the vinegar, olive oil, red pepper flakes, and salt, then stir. Let it sit while you cook the steak so the flavors can mingle.
  2. Pat the steaks dry with paper towels. Season both sides with the salt and black pepper.
  3. Heat a 10 to 12 inch skillet over medium-high heat for 3 minutes until very hot. Add the oil and swirl.
  4. Lay the steaks in the pan and leave them alone for 4 minutes so a crust forms.
  5. Flip the steaks and cook 3 to 4 minutes more. For medium-rare, pull them when an instant-read thermometer reads 130 to 135 F in the center.
  6. Move the steaks to a cutting board and rest them 5 minutes. Slice against the grain.
  7. Spoon about half the chimichurri over the sliced steak and serve the rest on the side.
Side: Optional simple side: a bagged salad or a warm slice of bread. Keeps vegetables limited.
Chef's tip: Season the steaks right before they hit the pan, not an hour early. Salt drawn out too early can leave the surface damp and slow the crust.
Shortcut / prep ahead: Chimichurri can be made up to 2 days ahead. Store it in a sealed jar in the refrigerator and let it sit 15 minutes at room temperature before serving. Do not leave garlic-in-oil at room temperature.
Leftovers: Refrigerate sliced steak within 2 hours and use within 3 days. Reheat gently or eat cold over salad.
Food safety: Steak is cooked to the owner's preferred doneness. Use an instant-read thermometer and rest before slicing.

Pesto Shrimp Pasta with Cherry Tomatoes

Plump shrimp tossed with a spoonful of basil pesto, blistered cherry tomatoes, and linguine. A Hall of Fame favorite, back for an encore.
Protein: Shrimp Servings: 2 (for two)
Difficulty: Easy Cleanup: Easy (one pot, one skillet)
Prep 5 min  |  Cook 13 min  |  Rest 0 min  |  Total 18 min
Ingredients for two
Pasta and sauce
  • 6 ounces dried linguine (about half a standard 12-ounce box)
  • 1 tablespoon kosher salt for the pasta water
  • 3 tablespoons basil pesto, jarred or homemade
  • 1/4 cup reserved pasta water
  • 2 tablespoons finely grated Parmesan
Shrimp and tomatoes
  • 10 ounces raw large shrimp (16 to 20 count), peeled and deveined, thawed
  • 1 tablespoon extra-virgin olive oil
  • 1 cup cherry tomatoes, halved
  • 1 medium garlic clove, minced
  • 1/4 teaspoon kosher salt
  • 1/8 teaspoon black pepper
  • Pinch of red pepper flakes (optional)
Directions
  1. Bring a large pot of water to a boil. Add the salt and the linguine. Cook 1 minute less than the package directions for al dente.
  2. While the pasta cooks, pat the shrimp dry and season with the salt and pepper.
  3. Scoop out 1/4 cup of the pasta water, then drain the pasta.
  4. Heat the olive oil in a large skillet over medium-high heat. Add the shrimp in one layer and cook 1 to 2 minutes per side until they turn pink and opaque and curl into a loose C. Move them to a plate.
  5. Lower the heat to medium. Add the tomatoes and cook 2 minutes, until they start to soften and blister. Add the garlic and stir 30 seconds.
  6. Add the drained pasta, pesto, and reserved pasta water. Toss for 1 minute so the pesto coats each strand. Stir in the shrimp and any juices.
  7. Top with the Parmesan and pepper flakes if using, then serve right away.
Side: None needed. The tomatoes are the vegetable.
Chef's tip: Save the pasta water before draining. A quarter cup of starchy water is what turns pesto into a silky coating instead of a thick smear.
Shortcut / prep ahead: Thaw shrimp overnight in the refrigerator, or in a bowl of cold water for 15 minutes. Halve the tomatoes up to a day ahead and keep them refrigerated.
Leftovers: Refrigerate within 2 hours and use within 2 days. Reheat with a splash of water in a skillet over medium heat.
Food safety: Shrimp must be opaque and cooked through, with no translucent centers.

Honey-Garlic Pork Chops

Golden pan-seared pork chops finished in a glossy honey, soy, and garlic glaze. A 4-star favorite that is ready to come back.
Protein: Pork loin chops Servings: 2 (for two)
Difficulty: Easy Cleanup: Easy (one skillet)
Prep 5 min  |  Cook 14 min  |  Rest 3 min  |  Total 22 min
Ingredients for two
Pork
  • 2 boneless center-cut pork loin chops, about 3/4 inch thick (about 5 to 6 ounces each)
  • 1/2 teaspoon kosher salt
  • 1/4 teaspoon black pepper
  • 1 tablespoon olive oil
Glaze
  • 1 tablespoon butter
  • 3 medium garlic cloves, minced
  • 3 tablespoons honey
  • 2 tablespoons low-sodium soy sauce
  • 1 teaspoon apple cider vinegar
  • 1/4 cup low-sodium chicken broth
  • 1 green onion, sliced (for garnish)
Directions
  1. Pat the chops dry and season both sides with the salt and pepper.
  2. Heat the olive oil in a 12 inch skillet over medium-high heat until shimmering. Add the chops and cook 3 to 4 minutes, until deep golden underneath.
  3. Flip the chops and cook 3 to 4 minutes more. Check the thickest part with an instant-read thermometer. It must reach 145 F. Move the chops to a plate.
  4. Lower the heat to medium. Melt the butter in the same skillet, then add the garlic and stir 30 seconds until fragrant.
  5. Whisk in the honey, soy sauce, vinegar, and broth. Simmer 2 to 3 minutes until the sauce thickens slightly and coats a spoon.
  6. Return the chops and any juices to the skillet. Spoon the glaze over them and turn once to coat.
  7. Let the chops rest 3 minutes in the skillet or on a plate, so they stay juicy and the temperature holds at 145 F or higher. Garnish with green onion and serve.
Side: Optional: a bagged salad, or a scoop of microwave rice to catch the glaze.
Chef's tip: Glaze only at the end. Honey and garlic scorch if they sit in a hot pan, so lower the heat before adding the glaze.
Shortcut / prep ahead: Mix the glaze up to 2 days ahead and refrigerate it in a jar. Warm it for 20 seconds in the microwave before using.
Leftovers: Refrigerate within 2 hours and use within 3 days. Reheat gently to 165 F.
Food safety: Pork must reach 145 F at the thickest point and rest 3 minutes before serving.

Harissa-Honey Chicken Thighs with Couscous

Boneless thighs glazed with smoky harissa and honey, served over fluffy couscous with a cool yogurt drizzle. A fresh cuisine for the rotation.
Protein: Chicken thighs Servings: 2 (for two)
Difficulty: Easy Cleanup: Easy (one skillet, one small pot or bowl)
Prep 5 min  |  Cook 14 min  |  Rest 3 min  |  Total 22 min
Ingredients for two
Chicken
  • 1 pound boneless, skinless chicken thighs (about 4), trimmed
  • 1 1/2 tablespoons harissa paste (adjust for your heat preference)
  • 1 tablespoon honey
  • 1 teaspoon smoked paprika
  • 2 medium garlic cloves, minced
  • 1/2 teaspoon kosher salt
  • 1 tablespoon olive oil
Couscous and finish
  • 1/2 cup instant couscous
  • 1/2 cup boiling water
  • 1/4 teaspoon kosher salt (for the couscous)
  • 2 tablespoons plain Greek yogurt
  • 1 tablespoon chopped fresh parsley
Directions
  1. In a bowl, stir together the harissa, honey, paprika, garlic, and salt. Add the chicken thighs and turn to coat.
  2. Heat the olive oil in a 12 inch skillet over medium-high heat for 2 minutes.
  3. Lay the thighs in the pan and cook 5 to 6 minutes without moving them until well browned on the bottom.
  4. Flip and cook 5 to 6 minutes more. The thickest part must reach 165 F on an instant-read thermometer.
  5. While the chicken rests 3 minutes on a cutting board, make the couscous. Put it in a bowl with the salt, then pour the boiling water over it. Cover for 5 minutes, then fluff with a fork.
  6. Slice the chicken and spoon it over the couscous. Drizzle with yogurt, sprinkle with parsley, and serve.
Side: None needed. The couscous is the starch and the chicken is the main.
Chef's tip: Add the honey only in the last minute of cooking or after the chicken comes out. Honey burns fast over high heat.
Shortcut / prep ahead: Coat the raw chicken with the harissa mixture up to 24 hours ahead and keep it covered in the refrigerator. Add the oil and cook when ready.
Leftovers: Refrigerate chicken and couscous separately within 2 hours and use within 3 days. Reheat chicken to 165 F.
Food safety: Chicken must reach 165 F. Wash the cutting board and hands after handling raw chicken.

Smart 10-Minute Prep-Ahead Plan

  • Sunday evening (10 minutes): Make the chimichurri and store it in a sealed jar in the refrigerator. Halve the cherry tomatoes and refrigerate them in a container. Move the shrimp from the freezer to the refrigerator to thaw.
  • Monday evening (5 minutes): Coat the chicken thighs with the harissa mixture and refrigerate them covered. Mix the honey-garlic glaze ingredients (but do not add the garlic until cooking).
Food-safe storage: Keep raw chicken, pork, and shrimp on their own plates and away from ready-to-eat foods. Use a clean cutting board, or wash it with hot soapy water between raw meats and vegetables. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours. Discard anything older than 3 to 4 days. Do not leave chimichurri (garlic in oil) on the counter for more than 2 hours.

Check Your Pantry and Refrigerator First

  • Look for olive oil, neutral oil, kosher salt, and black pepper.
  • Check for harissa paste, honey, low-sodium soy sauce, couscous, and chicken broth.
  • Check the refrigerator for basil pesto, Parmesan, Greek yogurt, and butter.
  • Check for a partial bag of frozen shrimp or a box of linguine from an earlier week.

Ingredient Reuse and Waste Reduction

  • Buy one 12-ounce box of linguine and use 6 ounces. The rest keeps for a future dinner.
  • Use the entire parsley bunch: chimichurri this week and garnish with the stems trimmed for the chicken.
  • A pint of cherry tomatoes gives 1 cup for the pasta; use the rest in salads or sandwiches.
  • Use the chimichurri on eggs, chicken, or sandwiches later in the week.
